# YouTube monetization policies for an AI-assisted, templated Hindi horror-comedy cartoon channel (as of late Nov 2026)

> **How this was researched.** support.google.com, tubefilter.com, techcrunch.com, socialmediatoday.com and vidiq.com could not be fetched from this session because the network allowlist blocks them. Every finding below comes from web-search result text: snippets and summaries of those pages, plus some secondary blogs. Quotes from the YouTube Help Center were seen only as search snippets, so check the exact wording on the live pages before relying on it. Dates are given wherever the sources give one. "Anecdotal" marks creator or vendor-blog claims.

## 1. Inauthentic content policy (formerly "repetitious content")

### Takeaway
The July 15, 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content." It targets mass-produced or template-driven videos with little variation, not AI itself. A July 2026 clarification split non-monetizable content into three buckets: generic/repetitive, unsatisfying/off-putting, and AI personas on sensitive topics. YouTube said this was not a new rule. The Help Center explicitly allows a **series that follows a set of recurring characters** if **each video has a distinct storyline**. That is the deciding line for this channel. Code-drawn templates are fine. Templated *stories* are the risk.

### Cited Findings
- **July 2025 (announced about July 9, effective July 15, 2025):** YouTube called it a "minor update" to long-standing YPP policies. The main change renamed "repetitious" to "inauthentic" to make clear the policy covers mass-produced or repetitive content — [TechCrunch, 2025-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-clarifies-july-15-policy-update-repetitious-content-rebranded-as-inauthentic-content/)
- **Examples YouTube cited (2025):** channels that upload *narrated stories with only superficial differences between them*, and *slideshows that all have the same narration*. YouTube said the list is not exhaustive — [Search-result summary of TechCrunch/SMT coverage](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- **Help Center wording (seen as a search snippet):** content "that looks like it's made with a template with little to no variation across videos, or content that's easily replicable at scale." It also lists "videos that reuse a highly similar storyline template, with the same outcome" and "AI-generated content made with generic templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective" — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/youtube-cracks-down-on-inauthentic-content-with-monetisation-policy-update-what-new-guidelines-say/2691562)
- **Allowed example (Help Center snippet):** "Similar content, like a series following a set of characters across episodes or a channel that does product reviews, but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept" — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- The policy applies to the **channel as a whole**: "If you have videos that violate our guidelines, monetization may be removed from your entire channel" — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison), July 2025:** he called it a "minor update" and confirmed that AI itself is not banned and that AI-using channels remain eligible. Reaction and commentary videos are not affected — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/never-fear-reaction-videos-are-still-allowed-under-youtubes-new-inauthentic-content-policy-222401009.html); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/); [Tubefilter, Jan 2026](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/)
- **Enforcement wave, Dec 2025 to Jan 2026:** a Kapwing report found at least 16–18 of the top AI-slop channels deleted or with their videos hidden (e.g., "Three Minute Wisdom" lost most of its content and "Imperio de Jesus" was removed). Counts differ between outlets — [Tubefilter, 2026-01-29](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [PiunikaWeb, 2026-01-12](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/01/12/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-enforcement-reports/)
- **India:** India has been described as the epicentre of the AI-content boom. The Indian channel "Bandar Apna Dost" (AI monkey videos, reportedly about $4.25M in revenue) was flagged by Kapwing as the most-viewed AI-slop channel and was reported as still thriving after the crackdown. Two channels behind AI fake movie trailers, one of them India-based, were demonetized and then terminated in late 2025 — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/indian-ai-monkey-channel-bandar-apna-dost-pulls-425m-ai-slop-floods-youtube-3793922); [The Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/technology/tech-news/ai-slop-channels-thrive-on-youtube-despite-crackdown-indian-channel-bandar-apna-dost-leads-the-surge-1035022); [Tribune PK, Jan 2026](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2582980/youtube-terminates-ai-generated-fake-movie-trailer-channels-after-policy-breaches); [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-demonetizes-channels-behind-ai-generated-movie-trailers/)
- **2026 priorities:** CEO Neal Mohan named AI slop a 2026 priority in his annual letter, pledging to build on spam and clickbait detection — [search summary of Tubefilter/SEJ coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtubes-ai-slop-problem-and-how-marketers-can-compete/)
- **July 2026 clarification (about July 13–20, 2026):** the guidance now names three non-monetizable categories:
  1. **generic or repetitive content**
  2. **unsatisfying or off-putting content**, meaning emotionally manipulative formulas, formats copied until videos feel interchangeable, and shock or surprise used *purely* to attract views. Examples given were stitched-together AI clips with no narrative used only to surprise, and realistic fake celebrity-death footage.
  3. **AI personas/"experts" on sensitive topics** such as health, finance and legal.

  YouTube and Tubefilter framed this as "not a new policy"; the rules on monetization "have not changed" — [Tubefilter, 2026-07-13](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [TechCrunch, 2026-07-20](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-unsatisfying-or-off-putting-videos-with-demonetization-3387786/); [Android Headlines, July 2026](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/07/youtube-monetization-rules-ai-slop-inauthentic-content.html)
- **Conflicting details on the 2026 update:** some creator blogs say the guidance went live July 16, 2026, and one says the policy was renamed again to "Generic or Repetitive Content." Not verified against YouTube — [creatorblade](https://creatorblade.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026-stay-monetized); [newmoneymatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/); [outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026)
- **Collateral damage (June 2026):** reports say legitimate faceless creators, including fully human-made channels, were hit by demonetization waves (anecdotal) — [The Next Web, 2026-06-15](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/youtubes-war-on-ai-slop-collides-with-faceless-creators-who-built-the-platform-first/)
- **Misleading headline to watch for:** MediaNews4U ran "AI-driven visuals, voices and deepfakes barred from YouTube monetization," but its own text describes the July 2025 demonetization update, not a ban. Its dates are also inconsistent — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/ai-driven-visuals-voices-and-deepfakes-barred-from-youtube-monetization/)

### Inferences
- The channel's production stack (code-drawn templates, reusable sets, TTS, AI co-written scripts) is not disqualifying by itself. What reviewers judge is the **output**: do episodes look and feel interchangeable?
- The biggest single risk is a **"same storyline template, same outcome"** pattern, for example every Short being "Bhoot scares Dadi → Dadi out-scares Bhoot → punchline." Episodes need clearly different premises, settings, conflicts and endings, and running arcs help.
- The "unsatisfying/off-putting" bucket matters for horror. Jump scares inside a narrative comedy are fine. Bare jump-scare clips with no story would match the "shock purely for views" example.
- The Bandar Apna Dost example shows that AI character channels from India can stay monetized, but it is not a safe template to copy. It was the named poster child of "AI slop."

### Gaps
- I could not read the full live text of Help page 1311392 to confirm the exact current allowed and not-allowed example lists, or whether the section was renamed in July 2026.
- I found no documented case of an *animated series with recurring characters* being specifically approved or rejected under this policy after July 2025.

## 2. Reused content: compilations of the creator's own Shorts

### Takeaway
The reused content policy targets material taken *from others* (or not clearly made by you) without significant added value. Compiling **your own** original Shorts is not reused content in the copyright-free-ripping sense. However, a channel dominated by compilations can look "repetitive/low-effort" to reviewers. Separately, since Oct 2025, YouTube has reduced *recommendation reach* for reposted or duplicate Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- Reused content means repurposing "content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value." It applies "even if you have permission from the original creator" — [YouTube Help 1311392 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- It is a **channel-level** policy: "if we cannot clearly tell that you made the content, monetization may be removed from your entire channel." Reviewers may check videos, channel description and video titles — [YouTube Help 1311392 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Compilations are explicitly in scope. Compilations of clips from other sources with minimal narration are the textbook violation — [vidIQ reused-content guide (vendor)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/understanding-youtubes-reused-content-policy)
- The July 2025 update did **not** change the reused content policy — [search summary of SMT/NetInfluencer coverage](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-clarifies-july-15-policy-update-repetitious-content-rebranded-as-inauthentic-content/); [newmoneymatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/)
- **Oct 2025:** YouTube changed recommendations to favor original Shorts over reposted clips. This was a recommendation change, not a new monetization rule — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/phones/youtube-cracks-down-on-reposted-shorts-with-a-new-recommendation-update/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/youtube-ai-shorts-duplicate-content-460353)
- Advice to reviewers' targets (vendor blog, anecdotal): rejection notes describe patterns such as "a series where only the title changes." Creators who script, voice and edit their own work and still get rejected should appeal and show their process. Reapply 30 days after a first rejection and 90 days after later ones — [AIR.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected)

### Inferences
- Compiling your own Shorts into a long video is defensible. To make it clearly acceptable: add **new framing material** (a fresh intro or outro, new linking scenes, a narrator or "Dadi's commentary" segments, a story order that builds a mini-arc, bonus or unreleased scenes), use a new title and description, and keep compilations a **minority** of long uploads. Reviewers weigh newest videos and the largest share of watch time; if those are compilations, the channel reads as repackaging.
- Better options: make the weekly long video an original episode, or alternate original long episodes with "season recap plus new ending" compilations.

### Gaps
- No official threshold for how much self-compilation is acceptable. No official statement specifically about compiling your *own* Shorts.

## 3. AI voices / text-to-speech narration

### Takeaway
No official YouTube text bans TTS or AI voices for monetization. The risk comes from TTS combined with repetitive or low-value content, which matches YouTube's own example of "slideshows with the same narration." Third-party guides consistently say a synthetic voice on repetitive content will not pass, and human voice or creative input improves the odds. Most of that guidance comes from AI-voice vendors, so it carries a conflict of interest.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's 2025 example of inauthentic content: "slideshows that all have the same narration" and "narrated stories with only superficial differences" — [TechCrunch, 2025-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows)
- Vendor and secondary claim: AI voices/TTS are "NOT banned," but "simply adding a synthetic voice to repetitive content will not meet monetization standards." Ritchie reportedly said the update targets content "made with a template with little variation" or "easily replicable at scale." Vendor sources, and the verbatim Ritchie quote was not verified — [Typecast](https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/); [Resemble AI](https://www.resemble.ai/ai-voice-youtube-videos-monetize/); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-ai-monetization/)
- "YouTube evaluates the output, not the tool. Mass-producing identical content isn't [ok]" (vendor guide) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)
- The July 2026 update's AI-persona bucket targets synthetic "experts" (AI faces/voices presented as doctors, financial advisers and similar) in sensitive niches. It does not cover fictional cartoon characters — [newmoneymatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/); [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/07/youtube-monetization-rules-ai-slop-inauthentic-content.html)

### Inferences
- Kokoro TTS voices for fictional characters (Dadi, Bhoot) is closer to "voice acting" than "AI narrator reading a script." It is lower risk than a single monotone narrator, *provided* each character has a distinct voice and there are dialogue, timing, music and sound design.
- Practical hedges: give characters distinct voices and emotion, add human-performed elements (a human-recorded intro or outro, a recurring human-voiced character or creator tag, human laughs or reactions), and avoid one flat narrator voice reading stories.
- "Meaningful human contribution" has no official definition. The working interpretation from YouTube's examples is original storyline, creative choices and variation that a viewer can perceive.

### Gaps
- I found no official YouTube statement specifically about TTS, and no verifiable reports of TTS-voiced *animated character* channels being rejected or approved. No information was found on how Hindi TTS quality affects reviewer perception.

## 4. Altered or synthetic content disclosure label

### Takeaway
Disclosure is required for **realistic** altered or synthetic content that viewers could mistake for real people, places or events. Clearly unrealistic content such as stylized 2D cartoons generally does not require it. Disclosing does **not** reduce reach or monetization, per YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- Creators must disclose "meaningfully altered or synthetically generated content that seems realistic" — [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- No disclosure is required for "clearly unrealistic content, such as animation" (March 18, 2024 launch) — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [9to5Google, 2024-03-18](https://9to5google.com/2024/03/18/youtube-altered-content-disclosure/)
- Nuance: the support page reportedly lists a realistic AI-generated element inside an animated video (an AI missile in a fully animated video) as needing disclosure, so animation is not automatically exempt if it contains realistic synthetic depictions — [YouTube Help 14328491 (search summary)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- Disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Failing to disclose when required can lead to penalties, and YouTube may add labels itself, including automatically detected ones — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [Scripps News](https://scrippsnews.com/stories/youtube-requiring-creators-to-label-realistic-ai-content/)
- A later update moved the label for photorealistic content to a more prominent position. Labels for non-photorealistic or animated AI content may appear in the expanded description — [YouTube Blog: improving AI labels](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/); [YouTube Help 15447836](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A code-drawn 2D horror-comedy cartoon with TTS voices likely does **not** require the label. A "Yes" would only be needed if a video used a realistic synthetic voice of a real person or realistic depictions of real events. If unsure, disclosing costs nothing per YouTube. Mentioning "animated with our own code-based pipeline; voices via TTS" in the About section signals transparency to reviewers.

### Gaps
- The date of the most recent label-placement change could not be confirmed. There is no official data on whether labels affect viewer CTR.

## 5. Made for kids (MFK) classification

### Takeaway
Animation alone does not make content "made for kids." Mature themes such as violence and scary content point toward "not made for kids," and content aimed at teens or adults should be set "not made for kids." However, a cute-looking cartoon featuring a grandmother and a ghost has kid appeal, so the channel must show a clear teen/adult orientation in its themes, language, humor and metadata. MFK status disables comments, notifications and personalized ads, which significantly reduces revenue.

### Cited Findings
- FTC/COPPA factors: subject matter, visual content, animated characters or child-oriented activities, music or audio, age of models, and others. "Just because your video has bright colors or animated characters doesn't mean you're automatically covered by COPPA". The FTC recognizes "animated programming that appeals to everyone" (FTC blog, Nov 2019) — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children?page=11)
- YouTube: content with "sexual themes, violence, obscene or other mature themes not suitable for young audiences" is likely not MFK. General-audience content, including content "intended for a teen or older audience," should be set "not made for kids" — [YouTube Help: Determining if your content is made for kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en-GB)
- A "13+" disclaimer is not decisive. YouTube's systems may not catch what the FTC considers MFK. YouTube Analytics is not designed to determine child-directedness — [YouTube Help FAQ 9684541](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children?page=11)
- MFK consequences: comments and notifications are off, no personalized ads (contextual ads only), no end screens or cards, no memberships, no Super Chat, no merch, no autoplay on home, and more. YouTube notes this can reduce revenue — [YouTube Help 9632097](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097); [YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)
- YouTube can override a creator's setting if it detects misclassification ([YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654), per the general audience-setting guidance; not re-verified in this session).

### Inferences
- Risk factors for this channel: cartoon visuals, a grandmother character, a ghost character, and slapstick could all read as kid-directed. To lower the risk: write teen/adult humor (family politics, in-law and adult life jokes, pop-culture references), put "horror comedy for teens and adults" positioning in the About section and descriptions, avoid nursery-rhyme music and kid-style thumbnails or titles ("for kids," "cartoon for children," "bachchon ki kahani"), and set the channel to "No, not made for kids."
- The FTC is a US regulator. India's DPDP Act 2023 also has children's-data provisions, but YouTube's MFK system is global and driven by COPPA.

### Gaps
- No source addresses animated horror-comedy aimed at teens specifically. The DPDP Act's interaction with YouTube MFK was not researched.

## 6. Advertiser-friendly guidelines: horror, violence, shocking content

### Takeaway
Fictional or dramatized horror is not inherently "limited ads." Yellow-icon risk comes from graphic or severe dramatized injuries, gore, and shock in the **thumbnail or first seconds**, and from "shocking content aimed towards kids." Mild cartoon scares with no gore should usually get full ads, but this is an inference. I could not retrieve the exact horror-specific wording.

### Cited Findings
- All content must meet Community Guidelines plus program policies. Violations can lead to "limited or no ads" — [YouTube Help: Advertiser-friendly content guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- "Dramatized displaying severe and shocking injuries" appears among the limited-ads categories. Gaming examples: content "manufactured to create a shocking experience" earns no ads, and graphic violence in the thumbnail or first seven seconds earns no ads — [YouTube Help 6162278 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278)
- There is a separate category for **shocking content aimed at kids**. Some advertisers prefer family-friendly content, which can lead to limited ads — [YouTube Help 6162278 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en-GB)
- A 2026 update made dramatized, non-graphic content on controversial issues eligible for ad revenue — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-ad-placement-rules-related-to-dramatic-content/)
- Advertisers using Google Ads "Standard" inventory are less likely to see ads on content with strong dramatized violence. This is a separate, advertiser-side control that can reduce fill without a yellow icon — [Google Ads: content suitability](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12764663?hl=en)
- Creators can request human review of automated monetization decisions — [YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278)

### Inferences
- Keep scares comedic and bloodless: no visible wounds, gore, dismemberment or realistic suicide or self-harm imagery. Keep thumbnails and the first 7 seconds free of gore or graphic imagery. Avoid any framing of scary content as "for kids," which combines the MFK risk with the "shocking content aimed at kids" ad risk.
- Profanity in Hindi dialogue is judged like profanity in other languages. Mild language in the middle of a video is usually fine; heavy profanity in the opening or the title is risky. This is general AFG knowledge and was not re-verified here.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact current horror and violence wording (e.g., whether "horror" or "jump scares" are named). The date of the 2026 "dramatic content" update was not confirmed.

## 7. What YPP reviewers look at, and practical approval steps

### Takeaway
Officially, reviewers sample the **main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, and the videos with the biggest share of watch time**, plus metadata such as titles and the channel description. The working recipe: make those sampled videos the most original and varied, show human creative involvement, and keep compilations or near-duplicates out of the most-viewed and newest sets. Eligibility thresholds double for new applicants from Feb 1, 2027, so timing matters.

### Cited Findings
- "Since they can't check every video, our reviewers may focus on your channel's main theme, most viewed videos, newest videos, biggest proportion of watch time" — [YouTube Help 1311392 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reviewers may also check the channel description and video titles (official). Thumbnails and the About section appear only in third-party guides — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/get-monetized-youtube/)
- **Threshold change (announced Aug 10, 2026, effective Feb 1, 2027):** new applicants need 8,000 public watch hours in 12 months (was 4,000) or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days (was 10M). The 1,000-subscriber requirement is unchanged. Channels already in YPP are unaffected — [Tubefilter, 2026-08-10](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/); [NewscastStudio, 2026-08-10](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/); [9to5Google, 2026-08-10](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/10/youtube-premium-lite-expansion-monetization-changes/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/new-youtube-creators-face-8-000-hour-bar-for-ad-revenue-from-february-2027/)
- **From Feb 1, 2027:** to receive *Shorts* feed ad and Premium revenue, channels need 10M qualified Shorts views in the prior 90 days. Cartoon Brew argues indie animators are hit hardest — [Cartoon Brew](https://cartoonbrew.com/shorts/youtube-shorts-monetization-rule-changes-265682.html); [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/youtube-sets-10-million-shorts-views-for-monetisation-from-2027-157245.html)
- If rejected: reapply after 30 days (first rejection) or 90 days (later ones), and appeal while showing your process (vendor blog, anecdotal) — [AIR.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected)

### Inferences: do/don't checklist for a human YPP review
**Do**
- Make every episode a distinct story (new premise, location, ghost type or folklore element, twist ending) and keep a running series bible. Use recurring characters, but don't repeat a beat-for-beat plot.
- Vary visuals within the template system: new sets, props, camera framing, character poses, effects and seasonal or festival episodes (Diwali, Holi, monsoon).
- Use distinct TTS voices per character with emotional variation, and add some human-performed audio (a creator intro or outro, a human-voiced character, human SFX or reactions).
- Show human authorship: an About section that explains the creative team, the writing process and the custom animation pipeline; occasional behind-the-scenes videos or Community posts (character sketches, script drafts, "how we animate with code"); a consistent channel brand and playlists by season or arc.
- Make sure the sampled set looks original. Before applying, the most-viewed, newest and top-watch-time videos should be original episodes, not compilations.
- Write unique Hindi titles and descriptions per video, with episode-specific summaries rather than boilerplate.
- Set audience to "Not made for kids" and position the channel explicitly for teens and adults.
- Keep scares comedic and bloodless, and keep thumbnails and openings gore-free.
- Use the AI disclosure only if something realistic is depicted. Otherwise "No" is accurate for stylized animation.
- Given the Feb 1, 2027 threshold doubling, apply before that date if the current thresholds are met (4,000 hours or 10M Shorts views plus 1,000 subscribers).

**Don't**
- Publish near-identical Shorts that differ only in dialogue lines or title.
- Make compilations the bulk of long uploads, or upload them without new framing material.
- Use a single flat TTS narrator reading stories over static or looping visuals, which is the "slideshow with same narration" pattern.
- Post jump-scare-only clips with no narrative ("shock purely for views").
- Use kid-oriented keywords, nursery-style music or "for kids" positioning.
- Post at a volume that outpaces the ability to vary stories. Daily Shorts are fine only if each one is genuinely different.

### Gaps
